# RabAI's character: what it was drawn from

A note for the rabbinic board about the core premises' section "The kind of teacher you are".
Draft, 13 October 2026.

## What Josh asked for

Josh asked that RabAI carry some of the essence of Rav Berkovits, a rabbi he describes as
kind-hearted and truly caring, extremely learned, and able to handle very difficult situations,
and of other rabbis who are gentle and kind in the same way, deeply learned, and who embody a
Torah life. He asked us to learn how the Rav acts, speaks and thinks from his public talks.

## Who we took him to be

We took "Rav Berkovits" to mean **Rav Yitzchak Berkovits**, Rosh Yeshiva of Aish HaTorah in
Jerusalem and founder of the Jerusalem Kollel. If Josh meant another rav, the qualities below still
describe the kind of teacher he asked for, and this note should be corrected.

## What RabAI does and does not do with this

- The section describes **qualities**, not a person. It names no rabbi, so the model never tries
  to imitate one.
- RabAI never claims to be Rav Berkovits or any rabbi, never speaks in his name, never says what
  he (or any living rabbi) would think or rule, and never quotes him unless a source it was given
  contains the words. This follows the core premises' rule against inventing a life story or a
  source, and the concern about geneivat da'at (misleading people) in the reviewers' notes.
- Nothing here changes what RabAI teaches. The sources and the board decide that; this section is
  about manner.

## What the public sources say about him

These descriptions come from public pages found by a web search on 13 October 2026. The pages
themselves could not be opened from our working environment, so each point is **as summarized
by the search, for a reviewer to confirm** before anyone relies on it.

- **His roles.** An American-born rosh yeshiva, rosh kollel and posek in Israel; after about ten
  years at the Mir, he taught halacha at the Aish HaTorah women's seminary from 1983, was the
  spiritual guide (mashgiach ruchani) of Aish HaTorah until 2001, founded the Jerusalem Kollel in
  2001 to train rabbis and outreach teachers, and became Rosh Yeshiva of Aish HaTorah in 2019.
  Described as the "unofficial posek" of the kiruv (outreach) world.
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzchak_Berkovits);
  [The Jerusalem Kollel](https://www.thejerusalemkollel.com/);
  [Aish.com, his inauguration](https://aish.com/rabbi-yitzchak-berkovits-to-be-inaugurated-as-rosh-yeshiva-of-aish-hatorah/)
- **An adviser people turn to.** The Jerusalem Kollel describes him as a posek and a ba'al eitzah
  (someone people come to for counsel) whose advice rabbis seek from around the world. Other
  pages call him a lucid speaker on halacha and Jewish thought, and a caring mentor to hundreds of
  people returning to observance.
  [The Jerusalem Kollel](https://www.thejerusalemkollel.com/);
  [TorahDownloads](https://torahdownloads.com/s-352-rabbi-yitzchak-berkovits.html);
  [Aish yeshiva shiurim page](https://yeshiva.aish.com/rav-yitzchak-berkovits-shiurim/)
- **The mitzvos between people.** Aish.com calls him a leading expert on the mitzvos between one
  person and another. His articles there include "Judging Others Favorably", "Concealing Hatred",
  "Taking Revenge" and "The Process of Change", and he is known for his shiurim on guarding one's
  speech (shmiras halashon). His kollel's curriculum on these mitzvos runs to several volumes of
  sources. [Aish.com author page](https://aish.com/authors/48865702/);
  [Torahdownloads](https://torahdownloads.com/s-225-rabbi-yitzchak-berkowitz.html)
- **Teaching from the Gemara to the halacha.** For the laws of Shabbos he arranged the Gemara and
  the commentators on each topic into source sheets that run from the Gemara to practical halacha,
  so learners can cover the main opinions without being overwhelmed. A student of his, Rabbi
  Yehonasan Gefen, put them into English for an online program.
  [Israel National News](https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/314985) (one of several promotional
  articles about that program)
- **Facing hard times with humility.** In a talk to Aish HaTorah's staff at the start of the
  coronavirus pandemic (March 2020), he spoke of how humbling it was for a world that had felt in
  control, and of recognizing that HaShem, not we, runs the world.
  [Aish.com](https://aish.com/rabbi-yitzchak-berkovits-on-the-coronavirus/);
  [NLE Resources, transcript](http://nleresources.com/2020/03/harav-yitzchak-berkovits-on-the-coronavirus/)
- **Self-examination rather than blame.** In 2016 he taught rabbis and educators about the
  self-examination (cheshbon hanefesh) needed after a scandal and tragedy, in order to help others
  grow. [NLE Resources](http://nleresources.com/2016/07/rabbis-supporting-rabbis-in-the-wake-of-a-scandal/);
  [TorahDownloads, "Moving Forward After Scandal"](https://torahdownloads.com/shiur-1012909.html)
- **His home.** He grew up in Boro Park, the son of Holocaust survivors, and has spoken of his
  mother as his first teacher of emunah.
  [Mishpacha, "Share the Light", August 2019](https://mishpacha.com/share-the-light/);
  [Aish.com, "A Jewish Mother's Wisdom"](https://aish.com/a-jewish-mothers-wisdom/)

His recorded shiurim are on TorahAnytime, Aish Audio, TorahDownloads and YUTorah. Listening to a
few, especially the question-and-answer sessions, would be the best way for a reviewer to check
whether the section captures the manner Josh meant.

## How the qualities in the section follow from this

| In the section | Drawn from |
| --- | --- |
| Learned, and clear because of it | Lucid teaching; sources arranged from the Gemara to the halacha so learners aren't overwhelmed |
| Listen first; ask a gentle question when details change the answer | The way of a ba'al eitzah, and of a posek for outreach rabbis, where the person's situation decides the answer |
| See everyone in the story; judge favorably; protect dignity | His teaching of the mitzvos between people: judging favorably, not hating in one's heart, not taking revenge, guarding one's speech |
| Calm and steady | Facing a crisis with humility and perspective |
| Practical and honest | A posek and adviser people come to for clear guidance |
| Humble; knows when to send a person on | Humility before HaShem; knowing one's limits |
| Responsibility, not blame | Self-examination after scandal and tragedy, rather than blaming others |
| In no one else's name | Our own rule: RabAI may never imitate or speak for a real rabbi |

## Questions for the board

- Is this the manner Josh meant, and does it fit what RabAI should be?
- Should Rav Berkovits's name appear anywhere outside these notes, for example in a description
  of RabAI? We suggest not, unless the Rav or the Jerusalem Kollel is asked and agrees.
- Should other rabbis be named here as models of the same manner, so the section isn't drawn from
  one person alone?
