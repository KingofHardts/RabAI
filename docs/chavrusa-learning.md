# Learning with RabbAI as with a chavrusa: 100 things a real chavrusa does

Josh asked (2026-11-05) for RabbAI to feel like learning with a real chavrusa: a fluid conversation
you can interrupt, the source brought up and marked while RabbAI talks about it, a line of Gemara
read and then talked over, quick and natural, and good teaching of anything you ask.

This page lists 100 things good chavrusas do, drawn from the research below, and says for each one
what RabbAI does now. **Built** means it is in the app (this round or before). **Next** means it is
planned and small. **Later** means it needs more work. **Waiting** means it needs a decision from
Josh or the rabbinic board.

What the research says, in short:

- Writers on havruta describe **three partners: two people and the text**. Each partner answers to
  the other and to the words on the page ([Project Zug](https://www.projectzug.org/havruta-resources),
  [Schechter](https://schechter.org/?p=784), [Hadar, Pedagogy of Partnership](https://mechonhadar.s3.amazonaws.com/PoP+source+sheets/PoP_AncientWisdomModernDayConversations.pdf)).
- Orit Kent names **six practices in three pairs**: listening and articulating, wondering and
  focusing, supporting and challenging ([Brandeis, Teaching the Practices and Skills of
  Havruta](https://www.brandeis.edu/mandel/jewish-values/teaching-havruta/teaching-practices-skills-havruta.html),
  Elie Holzer and Orit Kent, [A Philosophy of Havruta](https://hadar.org/torah-tefillah/books/philosophy-havruta)).
  Focusing keeps wondering from turning into wandering. Teachers report that supporting and
  challenging are the hardest: people fall back on "I like what you said"
  ([Pardes, eight educators](https://elmad.pardes.org/pcje/21st-century-learning/2015/08/reflections-on-bringing-havruta-into-the-classroom-eight-seasoned-educators-weigh-in/)).
- In the beis medrash, partners **read the text and commentaries aloud to each other**, then argue it
  out, and a stronger partner corrects and fills in ([Wikipedia, Chavrusa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavrusa)).
  The Gemara cares about the road to the answer, the back and forth (shakla v'tarya), as much as
  the answer ([JTA](https://www.jta.org/?p=1772552)). Review (chazara) makes it stay.
- In conversation, people answer each other after a gap of about a fifth of a second. Voice systems
  feel slow mostly because they wait a fixed time for silence; judging whether the words sound
  finished lets them answer sooner without cutting people off
  ([Decagon](https://decagon.ai/glossary/what-is-semantic-turn-detection),
  [Lorikeet](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/blog/if-your-voice-ai-cant-wait-it-cant-listen),
  [Twig](https://www.twig.so/blog/voice-ai-agents-latency-budget-800ms)). These are company blogs,
  so their exact numbers should be taken lightly; the direction is what we used.

## The text is the third partner

1. **The source is on the table.** When RabbAI quotes a passage, it appears above the answer the
   moment RabbAI quotes it, in Hebrew and English, from the library. Built (this round).
2. **Pointing at the words.** The words RabbAI is quoting are marked on that passage while it talks.
   Built (this round): the marks come from RabbAI's own citations, each checked against the passage
   the same way the final answer's sources are.
3. **The mark follows the voice.** When the answer is read aloud, the mark moves to the quote or line
   being said at that moment. Built (this round).
4. **Several texts, one at a time.** When an answer uses the Gemara, Rashi and Tosafot, the card has
   a tab for each and follows whichever one RabbAI is talking about; you can pick a tab yourself.
   Built (this round).
5. **The library's own words.** RabbAI never copies a line out itself; the card and the lines it
   reads come from the library. Built (this round).
6. **Open it properly.** "Open in the reader" takes you to the passage in its book. Built.
7. **See the page as printed.** An answer about the Gemara offers its amud as printed. Built.
8. **Go back to the words when you disagree.** RabbAI is asked to settle a difference by looking at
   the line again, not by arguing past you. Built (core premises, this round; waits for the board).
9. **Out of the way when you want.** "Hide" folds the card to one line. Built (this round).
10. **The book's standing comes along.** A debated book's caution shows on the card beside its text.
    Built (this round).
11. **Not yet approved, said plainly.** Every passage from the testing library says it isn't yet
    approved by the rabbinic board. Built.
12. **The commentaries in their order.** Halacha questions open a seif with the commentaries around
    it, in the order the board will set. Built (proposed order).

## Reading a line, then talking about it

13. **Read a line, then talk.** RabbAI brings up one line (it is shown and read aloud from the
    library), then talks about it. Built (this round).
14. **Plain meaning first.** After a line, what it says in plain words. Built (core premises).
15. **The one thing worth noticing.** Not everything; the one point that matters. Built.
16. **A question back.** Most turns end with a question you can answer. Built.
17. **One line at a time.** Never the whole page at once. Built.
18. **Translate as you go.** The Aramaic words with their meaning, a few at a time. Built.
19. **Name the move.** Question, answer, proof, challenge, resolution: RabbAI says which step a line
    is. Built (core premises, this round; waits for the board).
20. **What is bothering Rashi?** Before Rashi's answer, what in the line made him comment. Built
    (this round).
21. **Hebrew and English aloud.** You choose whether a line is read in English, or Hebrew then
    English (Settings). Built (this round).
22. **The line stays where it was read.** In the answer, the line sits where RabbAI read it, so the
    conversation reads like the learning did. Built (this round).
23. **Mark the line while it is read.** The line being read is marked, in the answer and on the
    card. Built (this round).
24. **Pick up from the last line.** A chavrusa session remembers the last line reached, now also the
    lines RabbAI read. Built.
25. **The flow of the sugya.** "Show the flow" colors the argument's steps on the printed page.
    Built (on request).
26. **The Gemara's terms.** Recurring terms (tanu rabanan, ta shema, kashya) explained when they
    appear. Built (core premises).
27. **The tune.** Chavrusas read with a learning tune that marks questions and answers. Later: it
    would need recordings or a voice that can do it well.
28. **Read it yourself, then compare.** You read a line out loud and RabbAI tells you how you read
    it. Later: needs dependable speech recognition of Hebrew and Aramaic.

## Listening and saying it back

29. **Let them read first.** In the partner way, RabbAI asks you to read and explain the next line.
    Built.
30. **Say it back.** RabbAI says back what you said before adding to it. Built.
31. **Answer what they said.** It starts from your actual point. Built.
32. **Check a misheard word.** In Talk, a strange word is checked ("Did you mean Berakhot?"). Built.
33. **Not done yet? Go on.** If you keep talking while RabbAI is thinking, it takes the question back
    and adds what you said. Built (this round; not on iPhones, which need a tap to listen).
34. **"And…" means you're not done.** A pause after "and", "the" or "um" waits longer before
    sending. Built (this round).
35. **A finished phrase goes quickly.** A pause after a finished-sounding phrase sends it at once.
    Built (this round).
36. **Hold on.** "Keep talking" stops the send while you think. Built.
37. **A slower pace if you want it.** Settings: "send what I said quickly / after a longer pause".
    Built (this round).
38. **Listening for meaning, not just silence.** A model that judges whether you finished, rather
    than word lists. Later: the word lists are a first step.

## Wondering and focusing

39. **Notice what is surprising.** RabbAI points out what is hard or odd in a line. Built.
40. **Ask the text's question.** It asks the question the text raises. Built.
41. **Come back to the words.** Wondering always returns to the page. Built.
42. **Think out loud, honestly.** "I'm not sure yet; let's see how Rashi reads it." Built (this
    round).
43. **One question at a time.** Built.
44. **The deep diver.** A way of learning that goes into the sugya's questions and answers. Built.
45. **Just the page.** A way that brings nothing beyond the page. Built.
46. **Connections only from the sources.** A word found elsewhere is named only when that passage
    was given and cited. Built.
47. **Stay on what you are learning.** A session keeps to its text unless you change it. Built.
48. **Notes in the margin.** You mark a thought on a line and come back to it. Next.

## Supporting and challenging

49. **Encourage every honest try.** Built.
50. **Wrong? Show where.** Kindly, in the text, and let you correct it. Built.
51. **Right? One step further.** A question that takes you further. Built.
52. **Not only praise.** RabbAI doesn't open answers with praise. Built.
53. **Disagree for the sake of the truth.** Kindly, never to win. Built (this round).
54. **Glad when you find something.** Built (this round).
55. **Hints before answers.** The questioner gives a small hint, then a bigger one. Built.
56. **"Just tell me."** Always works. Built.
57. **Both sides, fairly.** A machlokes is given each side's reasoning. Built.
58. **Ask your rav.** Practical questions always end with the note to ask your rav. Built.
59. **Devil's advocate on request.** "Argue the other side." Next: works when asked; a button would
    make it easier to find.
60. **Celebrate understanding.** A short "That's it, exactly!" Built (this round).

## Taking turns: the rhythm of a conversation

61. **Short turns.** Two to four sentences in a conversation. Built.
62. **Cut in any time.** By tap, or by talking (not on iPhones). Built.
63. **Pick up where it was cut off.** RabbAI is told the last words you heard. Built.
64. **Its voice starts with the first sentence.** The answer is read while it is written. Built.
65. **A short first sentence.** RabbAI is asked to start with a short sentence so the voice begins
    sooner. Built (this round).
66. **A quicker voice in Talk.** Talk uses ElevenLabs' quick model, streamed. Built (this round);
    "Listen" on an answer keeps the fuller voice.
67. **No lists out loud.** Built.
68. **Hebrew said plainly.** Built.
69. **"Your turn."** RabbAI hands the next piece over. Built.
70. **It doesn't hear itself.** Words matching what RabbAI is saying are ignored. Built.
71. **Silence is fine.** After a long quiet the microphone closes, kindly. Built.
72. **Questions said while it talks wait their turn.** Built.
73. **A ring, not a countdown.** In the quick pace, a short ring instead of "Sending in 2". Built
    (this round).
74. **Back-channel sounds.** "Mm-hm" while you talk, as people do. Later: it is easy to get wrong.
75. **Talking over each other.** Both speaking at once, as chavrusas do. Later: needs the
    microphone and voice to run together on every phone.
76. **iPhone without taps.** On iPhones each turn starts with a tap. Waiting: a transcription
    service would fix this (`docs/transcription-research.md`), and needs Josh's choice.

## Pace and speed

77. **Short follow-ups go straight to the sources.** Built.
78. **Warmed up before the first question.** Built.
79. **A quick model for conversation.** Built.
80. **The first words in about two seconds** for a follow-up in Talk. Built (measured 1.5 to 2
    seconds before this round's voice change).
81. **The voice starts sooner.** The quick voice model. Built (this round); to be measured live.
82. **Audio played as it streams in.** Playing the voice while it downloads, not after. Later: works
    in most browsers, but iPhones need care with silent mode.
83. **Start answering before you finish.** Begin thinking while you are still speaking. Later.
84. **Sources ready before the question ends.** Look up the line you are on while you talk. Later.
85. **Nothing waits on the screen.** The card and marks come with the words, not after. Built.
86. **Timings kept for the health check only.** Built.

## Remembering, review, finishing

87. **Where you stopped.** Built.
88. **What you learned together.** Built (on your device, and your account).
89. **What you found hard.** Built (core premises).
90. **A summary to review at the end (chazara).** Built.
91. **The reviewer.** A friendly quiz on what you learned. Built.
92. **Spaced review.** Come back to a line after a day, a week, a month. Next.
93. **Notice tiredness.** Slow down, sum up, offer to stop. Built (this round).
94. **A learning schedule.** Daf Yomi, the parsha, or your own plan. Built (in part).

## Teaching anything

95. **The big idea first, then build.** Built.
96. **An example from the sources.** Built.
97. **Check you're with it.** Built.
98. **Pictures.** Ask about a photo of a page or your notes. Built.
99. **Words.** Tap any word for its meaning. Built.
100. **The person comes first.** RabbAI adjusts to how you learn, from what you tell it, never
     anything else about your life. Built.
