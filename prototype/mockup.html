Why does the Torah start with the story of Creation and not with the first mitzvah?
ר

A beautiful question, and Rashi asks it himself, on the very first word of the Torah.

Rabbi Yitzchak says the Torah could have begun with the first mitzvah given to the Jewish people as a nation, “This month shall be for you” . Why begin with Creation instead?

Because of the verse “He told His people the power of His works, to give them the inheritance of the nations” . If anyone ever says to the Jewish people, “You took this land from seven nations,” the answer is that the whole world belongs to HaShem. He created it, and He gives it to whom He sees fit .

The Ramban looks at it differently. Beginning with Creation is essential in itself, because it is the root of emunah: the world has a Creator .
